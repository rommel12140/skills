# Section catalog and composition rules

The patterns are **recommendations**: relationships to draw from, each informed by an observed winner.
They are not a menu to exhaust on one page.
Choose a pattern because a section brief calls for it.
A comparison table, a searchable list, or a plain paragraph remains a valid answer when it fits the task; Awwwards provenance does not require replacing a familiar structure that works.

## Patterns

| Pattern | Use when | Composition and behaviour | Avoid when | Observed in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Subject-led opening | A person, work or object can establish identity immediately | One dominant subject, concise orientation, a clear route onward | The visitor cannot identify the offer without substantial explanation | Lando Norris (portrait and helmet with next-race information); Siena (one film fills the frame) |
| Physical scale demonstration | Size, handling or fit is central to the claim | The product beside a meaningful physical reference; copy interprets rather than repeats | The scale is artificial, misleading, or irrelevant to the buying concern | Opal Tadpole (a hand holding the camera) |
| Text with embedded evidence | A short explanation benefits from nearby visual detail | One continuous readable statement with small, specific supporting objects inline | Long-form reading, poor contrast, or details that become too small to read | Opal Tadpole's introductory explanation |
| Focused work sequence | A few projects can prove depth | Selected projects get substantial space, consistent identification and direct access | Visitors mainly need to find one known item in a large archive | Telha Clarke (three named projects before the archive) |
| Contextual image field | Range matters after focused examples | Images at intentional, unequal scale and spacing around an explicit archive destination | The images are interchangeable stock or the destination gets lost | Telha Clarke's grid around "All Work" |
| Product-family grouping | Visitors must understand distinct types of offer | Real categories with recognisably different evidence and spans | The categories were invented to fill three equal boxes | Fluid Glass (four product families) |
| Stable explanation with changing object | A mechanism or construction has meaningful stages | Labels or explanation hold still while the object changes state beside them | A static diagram works as well, or the interaction hides essential information | Opal Tadpole's exploded technical view; iyO's scroll-driven exploded view |
| Process with working evidence | How the work is delivered affects confidence or fit | Each stage paired with its own people, tools, site condition or document; image scale follows what must be seen | The steps restate generic verbs and have no supporting material | Fluid Glass process section |
| Two-way choice | Two genuinely distinct next tasks exist | Both choices get clear destinations and distinguishing evidence, placed where the visitor knows enough to choose | Most visitors need one next action, or the categories overlap | Lando Norris on-track and off-track routing |
| Collection as an interactive field | Exploring the collection is the main task | Current item identity, collection controls, global escape routes and a direct item destination stay legible in one frame | Search, exhaustive comparison or quick lookup is the main task | Siena Film Foundation (behind an Enter splash) |
| Evidence-led testimonial | A named account resolves a specific uncertainty | Real attribution and the concrete experience; readable excerpts with access to the full account | Only vague praise or unverifiable identities exist | Fluid Glass client accounts |
| Invitation to a real next step | A visit, conversation, booking or purchase now follows from the evidence | The destination or outcome with the practical information needed to act | The invitation arrives before the visitor knows why they would take it | Fluid Glass showroom video with address and visit action |
| Compact practical closure | The visitor has reached the end or wants another route | Useful contact and navigation destinations with enough context | The footer repeats the whole page or introduces new major claims | Most winners |

The catalog excludes glowing node networks, floating glass panels, decorative gradient fields, emoji icons, universal three-card feature rows, and numbered or coded section labels.
Telha Clarke's live page labels its sections 01 to 04; that labelling is deliberately not carried into this catalog.

## Composition rules

All **recommendations**.

1. **Name one dominant relationship per section before choosing geometry.**
   A comparison needs aligned evidence; a sequence needs order; a size claim needs a scale reference.
2. **Share alignment anchors across the page, then vary spans.**
   An asymmetric section still needs a reason for where each element starts and ends.
3. **Supporting text gets less spatial emphasis than the evidence it explains.**
   If both compete equally, revise the hierarchy or split the task.
4. **Design empty space as grouping and pacing.**
   State whether it separates concerns, protects a focal subject, marks a pause, or makes room for a transition.
5. **Keep captions and claims attached to their evidence.**
   A dramatic crop that removes the relevant detail defeats the section.
6. **Review every adjacent pair of sections.**
   Keep one useful anchor while changing relationship, density or scale when the job changes.
7. **Keep comparable items comparable.**
   Repetition inside a project or product collection is legitimate; repeated generic feature claims with interchangeable icons are not evidence.
8. **Place actions where the preceding material makes them reasonable.**
   A section can end in understanding rather than a button while persistent navigation carries the main route.
9. **Treat the hero as the page's opening promise.**
   Its successor advances or substantiates it; later sections do their own content-specific work rather than rebuilding the opening composition.
10. **Make each transition express continuity, contrast, cause or completion.**
    A small upward fade on every section expresses none of these.

No golden ratio, whitespace percentage, card count, reveal duration or section maximum is required.
Check every dimension with the real text and assets.

## Annotated examples

These are analytical reductions of observed pages, not copies of studio wireframes.
They keep relationships and reading order, not dimensions.

### Igloo Inc: few sections, depth inside each

Studio account: three sections (arrival, projects, external links), mapped in grey sketches.
Each project is an object encased in ice; the same block for every project looked too similar while scrolling, so each got its own form.
The links section forms a different particle model per destination.

```text
Small identity                                     Peripheral utility

                       PROJECT OBJECT
             Project name crosses its spatial field
                       PROJECT OBJECT

Supporting information                             Project destination
```

**Recommendation:** separate the stable collection grammar from each item's identifying silhouette.
A small section count is enough when the content rewards exploration inside each section.
Do not add service, statistics and testimonial bands to make a page look complete.
An immersive object still needs readable identification and an accessible route to its destination.

### Lando Norris: identity, then evidence, routing and participation

Observed (October 2026, about nine content beats in twelve DOM sections): portrait and helmet opening with next-race information; a personal message; a positioning statement; a horizontal photographic history; an on-track and off-track choice; the helmet collection with an onward link; a store feature; partners; social destinations; footer.
Studio account: the brief was his life on and off the track, with motion drawn from racing and his personality.

**Recommendation:** reserve the opening for a legible identity proposition, then let each later composition fit its material.
A collection can repeat; a routing choice can be balanced; an editorial history can change image sizes.
The page holds together through subject, alignment and navigation, not one repeated arrangement.
Put a split choice where visitors know enough to choose.

### Fluid Glass: product, visit and delivery need different evidence

Observed (October 2026): glazing introduction with architectural media; company introduction; four product families; showroom video invitation; featured projects; paired architectural imagery; client accounts; approach and contact.
Studio account: lead generation and the showroom's role in that journey are why the video got its own space; a dedicated section shows the process from coordination to installation through photography and video.

```text
Product browsing                           Showroom invitation

             Tall product photo            Large continuous video of the space
                         Short context
  Product photo      Product photo          Visit proposition     Address
                                                                  Visit link
```

**Recommendation:** choose evidence at the level of the visitor's concern.
Finished work establishes capability, process evidence explains collaboration, and the showroom supplies a concrete next step.
Keep the change from a dense collection, to an expansive place, to practical detail; the whitespace works because each has a different reading demand.

### Telha Clarke: selected projects become an account of the practice

Observed (October 2026): arrival; studio introduction; three selected projects; an image field around the full archive link; vision; method; contact.
Studio account: three or four highlighted projects flow into a contextual image grid; one changing widget acts as the primary call to action across the site.

```text
Focused project sequence
          |
          v
Small image             Large image
               Archive destination
       Image                         Image
          |
          v
Architectural setting + practice principles
```

**Recommendation:** use a broad image field after focused proof when range matters, keep a clear destination inside it, and do not make every image the same size.
Keep the relationship between proof, breadth and principle on mobile.

### Opal Tadpole: a physical fact supplies the composition

Observed in archived Awwwards captures: a hand holding the camera establishes scale; a text-led explanation carries small product details inline; a technical view holds specifications on the left while an exploded product changes on the right.

```text
Product-scale opening                       Explanation

Short proposition       Hand + device       Readable statement with
                                            small relevant product details

Clear product navigation                    Practical compatibility detail
```

**Recommendation:** let the evidence persuade.
A hand establishes size, a side-by-side establishes difference, an exploded view establishes construction.
Choose the relationship before choosing photography, illustration or prototype.
Keep a buying or product-navigation route available through a long explanation.

### Siena Film Foundation: the collection is the opening

Observed (October 2026): after an Enter splash and a short onboarding overlay, the homepage is one full-frame film with title, credits, recognition, critic quotes and an Explore action, plus a ticket-style film picker.
It is one browsing field with changing film states, not a section per film.
Studio account: the left menu is local navigation for the active page, the right panel is global navigation; the slider has poster and footage modes.

```text
Studio identity                         Current film / global navigation

Festival recognition       FILM IMAGE               Critical response
                           FILM IMAGE
Film identity and credits                            Explore this film
```

**Recommendation:** open on the artifact when the work alone orients visitors and exploration is the task.
Make the current item, collection controls and global escape routes explicit at wireframe stage.
An unfamiliar service may need orientation before it can support this structure.
Never impose an About, Services, Testimonials sequence on every portfolio.
Treat an entry gate as a cost the content must justify; prefer letting visitors reach the work directly.

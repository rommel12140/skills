# unconventional-routes

The standard route is where everyone else queues.
For growth work the owner wants the routes a resourceful founder takes: test demand before the paperwork, go around a closed door, ask for the work before the job is posted.
Those routes are welcome as long as they do not get the owner in trouble.

## Before you settle for the norm

1. Name the blocker in one sentence, such as "the portal rejects applicants without NZ experience".
2. List at least three routes other than the standard one before you recommend the standard one.
3. Rank the routes by expected effect and by risk. Base each estimate on evidence, such as past replies or the employer's own posts, and say when it is a guess.
4. Classify every route as below. Recommend the best fine route, and offer gray routes with their risk notes.

## The line

Never state something false to an employer, client or authority.
Never put the owner's visa, licence or reputation at real risk without the owner's explicit word.
False statements are what get people caught, and they are the real source of legal, visa and reputation trouble.

## Classify every route

**Fine.** Unusual, honest, and against no rule.
- Offer contract or freelance work first when the full-time role is closed.
- Build a small working thing for a target company and send it to the founder or hiring manager.
- Apply to stretch roles, with framing that names the real gap and how the owner will close it.

**Gray but defensible.** Honest, but it bends a norm, a platform rule or an informal expectation.
- Run a small market test before formal setup, such as a few pre-sales or one pilot client, to find out if anyone pays.
- Offer a paid trial week in place of the usual interview loop.
- Contact a decision maker directly when the advert says to apply only through the portal.

**Over the line.** Never propose these, and say so when asked.
- Invent experience: employers, titles, years, skills, degrees or references.
- Give a false yes to a screening question, such as work rights, a licence or a required number of years.
- Fake or alter a document, take work that breaks visa conditions, or deceive a customer about what they buy.

## For gray routes

Write three things for each gray route, then let the owner decide:

1. The concrete risk: what could go wrong, who could act on it, and what it would cost.
2. How to keep it small: a cap on scale, money or time, a record of what was done, and a point to stop.
3. The formal route it stands in for, and the signal to switch to it.

Money earned can count as work under a visa, so check the visa conditions before any paid test.
When the risk touches a visa, a licence or tax, name the exact document or official page to check.
Do not claim legal certainty, and do not take a gray route on the owner's behalf.
